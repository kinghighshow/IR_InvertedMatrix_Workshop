Petr Andreev: 2026 PSF Board Election Candidate Interview

My work increasingly focuses on turning technical education into real-world participation: research, open-source contribution, conference speaking, and leadership.</p><p>Before focusing on Python, I helped build an educational community of roughly 2,500 participants. I assembled an organizing team, secured external funding, and developed partnerships with companies, an endowment, and other organizations to support courses, competitions, speakers, and community programs.</p><p>These experiences shaped the model behind much of my work:<br />develop developers → develop contributors → develop community leaders.<br /></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">What would you bring to the PSF Board of Directors?</h3><p>I would bring experience building organizations and systems around technical communities.</p><p>I have worked on both sides of community development: developing individuals through education and mentorship, and building the infrastructure around them through organizing teams, institutional partnerships, external funding, events, and opportunities to lead.</p><p>I also bring an unusual combination of technical depth and community-building experience. I can communicate with CPython contributors about implementation-level problems, with educators about developing new talent, and with institutions and companies about partnerships and resources.</p><p>My international work across Europe and Asia gives me another useful perspective: the ability to listen across communities, identify problems that repeat across regions, and connect people who have already developed solutions.<br /></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">What motivated you to run for the PSF Board of Directors?</h3><p>One of the PSF’s strategic goals resonates particularly strongly with me: Develop the Next Generation of Python Developers.</p><p>I have spent years experimenting with this problem on a smaller scale and have seen students progress from learning advanced Python to researching its internals, contributing upstream, and presenting their work publicly.</p><p>I now want to work on the system behind that progression.</p><p>The PSF is uniquely positioned to connect communities, educational institutions, employers, sponsors, maintainers, and contributors. I am running because I believe these connections can make participation in Python easier to discover and more sustainable over the long term.<br /></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">What problem or challenge do you want to address if you are on the Board?</h3><p>Contributor retention.</p><p>Python attracts enormous numbers of users, but converting initial interest into years of meaningful contribution is much harder.</p><p>Open-source contribution competes with paid work, family, and other demands on people’s time. The question I want to work on is: how can the PSF make sustained contribution easier and more rewarding?</p><p>I would explore clearer contributor pathways, mentorship infrastructure, recognition, funded contributor time, and partnerships with employers and educational institutions.<br />I would also measure whether these mechanisms actually work: repeat contributions, retention, mentorship activity, increasing responsibility, and long-term participation.<br />The PSF does not govern CPython technically, but it can help create conditions in which contributors and maintainers are more able to stay.</p><p>------</p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Note from the election administrators:&nbsp;</h3><p>Want to learn more about this candidate or ask them a question?&nbsp;<br /><br />Check out their <a href="https://www.python.org/nominations/elections/2026-python-software-foundation-board/nominees/petr-andreev/" target="_blank">nomination statement</a>.<br /><br />Check out their <a href="https://discuss.python.org/t/psf-board-nomination-ama-petr-andreev/108676/" target="_blank">AMA thread on discuss.python.org</a>.</p>
