Our tiny but mighty team works hard to deliver the projects, services, and programs that allow Python to be the thriving, independent, community-driven language it is today. PyCon US 2026 was packed with 7 days of content, education, and networking for the Python community, including 6 Keynote Sessions, 90 Talks (including the Charlas Spanish track and the new Future of AI with Python and Trailblazing Python Security tracks), 20 Tutorials, 25 Posters, 25+ Sprint Projects, 123 Open Spaces, 62 Booths, and 83 Sponsors!</li><li><b>Powering the inaugural Python Packaging Council election:</b> We were excited to run the election that formed the <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/09/announcing-2026-python-packaging.html">first Python Packaging Council (PPC)</a>—a long awaited technical decision-making body! The PPC will work to develop interoperability specifications that govern how Python packages are built, distributed, and installed. It will also coordinate efforts among packaging tool maintainers, the Python core team, and the broader community. The PSF is proud to have provided the administrative, logistical, and communications support that got the new council up and running.</li><li><b>Safeguarding PyPI against emerging threats:</b> <a href="https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2026-04-16-pypi-completes-second-audit/" target="_blank">PyPI completed its second external security audit</a>, carried out by Trail of Bits and funded by the Sovereign Tech Agency. The review turned up fourteen findings, two of them high severity, and PSF PyPI Safety and Security Engineer Mike Fiedler has already fixed all but two low-risk items that need larger changes. The auditors also wrote custom CodeQL queries, now part of PyPI’s automated checks, so the next bug of the same kind gets caught before it ships. Additionally, PyPI now rejects new files on releases older than 14 days, which stops anyone from grafting a malicious wheel onto a version people already trust. Meanwhile, community reporters sent in just under 1,000 malware and abuse reports in Q1, with over 40% landing in automated quarantine, protecting the Python ecosystem faster than ever.&nbsp;</li><li><b>Developing and adopting the PSF’s 5 Year Strategic Plan:</b> We’re proud to share that the <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/09/announcing-psf-strategic-plan-2026.html" target="_blank">PSF Board adopted the PSF Strategic Plan 2026</a>, covering 2026 to 2031, at its July 2026 meeting! This plan took shape in the open: we <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/05/strategic-planning-at-psf.html" target="_blank">shared the high-level goals in May</a>, then <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/06/psf-strategic-plan-2026-draft-open-for.html" target="_blank">published the full draft in June</a> and invited the community to weigh in. Our new <a href="https://www.python.org/psf/strategy/" target="_blank">PSF Strategy page</a> is its permanent home, where we’ll publish annual review findings and updates so the community can follow our progress.&nbsp;</li><li><b>Sustaining the Python Security Response Team: </b>As the number of vulnerability reports and CVE advisories grew 3-4X, the mostly-volunteer <a href="https://devguide.python.org/security/psrt/" target="_blank">Python Security Response Team (PSRT)</a> grew and adapted with it. Following the approval of PEP 811, <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/02/join-the-python-security-response-team.html" target="_blank">13 new members joined the PSRT</a>, its first additions since 2023, and PSF Security Developer-in-Residence Seth Larson published a new security policy and threat model that helps coordinators quickly tell security-relevant reports from regular bugs. The team published 15 vulnerability advisories in Q1 and triaged and fixed a python.org API authentication vulnerability in under 48 hours, followed by a third-party audit by Trail of Bits.&nbsp;</li><li><b>Empowering the Python community through Fiscal Sponsorship:</b> We are proud to continue supporting our 20 fiscal sponsoree organizations with their initiatives and events all year round. The PSF provides 501(c)(3) tax-exempt status to fiscal sponsorees such as PyLadies and Pallets, and provides back office support so they can focus on their missions. Consider <a href="https://linktr.ee/thepsf" target="_blank">donating to your favorite PSF Fiscal Sponsoree</a> and check out our <a href="https://www.python.org/psf/fiscal-sponsorees/" target="_blank">Fiscal Sponsorees page</a> to learn more about what each of these awesome organizations is all about!</li><li><b>Introducing the new PSF Community Partner Program:</b> The PSF announced the introduction of the <a href="https://www.python.org/psf/community-partners/" target="_blank">PSF Community Partner Program</a> in 2026. This new program is designed as an “in-kind” way for us to support Python events and initiatives with non-financial assistance through the use of the PSF logo and name, as well as promotional support via sharing qualified posts on PSF official social media accounts. The PSF has welcomed almost 40 community events and initiatives to the program so far!&nbsp;</li><li><b>Serving our community with grants</b>: We brought back the PSF Grants Program in a restructured and more sustainable format after a pause in 2025. The PSF awarded approximately $60K over 44 grants awards across the world in 2026; supporting local conferences and workshops that keep Python growing and accessible to all. We also <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/03/applications-to-join-psf-meetup-pro.html" target="_blank">opened up applications</a> for the <a href="https://www.meetup.com/pro/python-software-foundation-meetups/" target="_blank">PSF's Meetup Pro Network</a>. We hope to continue grant funding rounds in the future and your participation in this year’s fundraiser fuels that effort!</li><li><b>Honoring community leaders:</b> We honored 10 PSF Community Service Award (CSA) recipients this year across&nbsp;<a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/05/announcing-psf-community-service-award.html" target="_blank">two</a> <a href="https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/10/announcing-psf-community-service-award.html" target="_blank">batches</a>. Abhijeet Mote, Elena Williams, Inessa Pawson, Kafui Alordo, Kalyan Prasad, Hugo van Kemenade, Kevin Horn, Maria Jose Molina Contreras, Paul Everitt, and Sarah Abderemane received CSAs in 2026 for their dedication, creativity, and generosity that help the PSF fulfill its mission to support and facilitate the growth of a diverse and international community of Python programmers.</li></ul>
